## Scene-by-Scene Shot Design Notes

### Scene 3

- Dolly follows the man; floor not shown, so track is usable 
- Man turns around, takes a few more steps, both characters stop and look up 
- Camera **tilts up slowly** and cuts at an undetermined point — plan to tilt all the way up and decide the cut in editing 

### Scene 4 (Boy Walking in Woods — High Angle)

- Follows directly from Scene 3's upward tilt; **tilt angle continuity** is critical — the next shot must pick up at roughly the same tilt angle to avoid a jump 
- Preferred composition: **high angle looking down** at boy walking through forest; location is a hiking area with uneven, downhill terrain 
- Shot will be largely **static** given the difficulty of moving equipment at height; boy hears his name, looks around briefly, then walks off screen 
- Discussed shooting at **higher frame rate (30fps, slowed to 24fps)** to achieve a subtle slow-motion effect — not full slow-mo (60fps would be too extreme)  — to be confirmed with Sam and Alex
- Tilt resolution options considered: 
    - Option A: Start high on trees, tilt down to reveal boy as shot progresses
    - Option B: Continue tilting upward from Scene 3 (risks compositional mismatch since camera would be above the boy)
    - Decision: lean toward Option A (start on trees, tilt down); exact match of tilt angle may be addressable in editing

### Scene 5 (Longest Scene — Multi-Location)

- **Shot 1:** Front-angle dolly of man and woman walking and talking; woman hurries off screen, dolly stops, man remains in frame briefly 
- **Shot 2:** Woman looking up (not at man — she turns back and looks into the distance or slightly down, as if something made her stop, not as a reaction to him); man enters from screen right, freezes  — described as the **"painting shot"**, held static 
- Static frame preferred for the painting shot, though it constrains composition flexibility; location's natural depth (tree clearing) is key 
- **Dolly upstairs concern:** Getting the Fisher dolly up a staircase may be impossible; alternatives include a smaller rental dolly or shoulder rig for that specific shot 
- **Shoulder rig reservations:** Ishmam noted shoulder rig produces an unsettled, hovering look inconsistent with the film's Tarkovsky-influenced, settled aesthetic 
- **Shots 4–7 (missing drawings):** Ishmam to hand-draw rough compositions for shots 4, 5, 6, 7 so Shafi can generate AI reference images 
    - Shot 6: Walking down stairs
    - Shot 7: Wide shot of characters walking left-to-right in park-like setting (functions as an insert over continuing dialogue); location must feel park-ish but not overtly so, and must match the previous location visually 
- **Shot 8 (woman and man, depth composition):** Woman sits on something low (log/stump level, not bollard height — bollard is too tall and puts her near standing height) ; man positioned lower on a slope so their faces are at similar elevation when camera pans between them 
    - Location options considered: cut-off tree stump near the first park bench area (rejected — would feel like returning to an earlier location) ; area where Ishmam and Ishiko were at different elevations (favored) 
    - May need to **bring a wooden stump as a production design element** 
- **Shot 9 (dolly in on woman, tilt down, pan to man):** Dolly pushes in slightly on woman, camera tilts down to reveal book/hands, then pans left to man's face at roughly the same elevation ; dolly tracks needed for grass terrain 
    - Lens choice matters — must keep man's face in frame during pan without cutting off; a longer lens risks clipping 

### Scene 6 (Bed in the Woods)

- Opens with a low-to-high **pedestal or tilt up** from the forest floor revealing the bed; boy stops at the edge 
- Location: far end of the interview woods area; may be possible to tripod this without a dolly 
- **Boy touching the bed:** Close-up from same angle, multiple takes 
- **Jumping sequence (one long shot attempt):** 
    - Boy climbs onto bed, camera begins tilting up during first 1–2 jumps
    - Camera holds static at sky level; boy jumps in and out of frame
    - Boy eventually spins/turns mid-air; after expected jump doesn't happen, camera tilts back down
    - Camera either parallel with bed or bed angled with boy parallel to camera — to be tested 
- **Cut vs. no-cut debate:** Preferred to attempt the entire sequence in **one shot**; if dolly can't reach location or the shot is too technically difficult, a **cut to a close-up insert of boy gripping the fabric** provides a natural break 
- **Final shot of scene:** Rückenfigur (boy seen from behind), boy looks back, camera holds, then **pans slowly to woman standing near trees in the distance** 
    - Woman should be close to the tree line, almost against the trees 

### Scene 7 (Bollards / Dialogue-Heavy Scene)

- Single static shot for most of the scene; heavy on dialogue — actors must memorize lines, particularly for this scene 
- Characters sit apart, converse; woman stands, turns away, man eventually crosses an implied barrier first, woman follows 
- **Blocking note:** During the argument climax, man should be turned **sideways (screen right)**, not fully with his back to camera 
- **Cut before Tarkovsky pan:** A cut to something is needed between the argument and the pan; cutting to the boy risks giving him a premature conclusion 
    - Alternative considered: cut to **soil/earth from earlier forest scene** (no boy, just the ground he was digging) 
- **Tarkovsky pan (final shot of scene):** Inspired directly by a specific Tarkovsky shot (~1 minute 36 seconds, single take) 
    - Camera positioned close to where man and woman sit, panning across the field 
    - First stop of pan: **empty bollards** (where characters previously sat — now vacant) ; problem is shooting back toward bollards means the road and houses are visible in background 
    - Solution options: move bollards to a different position in the field (symbolic/supernatural placement), or include bollards briefly in frame just before panning to the couple 
    - Tarkovsky technique noted: shot uses a **zoom lens** (zoomed in at start, zooms out for depth); layered image composition (grass / dirt / trees / secondary trees / sun) 
    - Acknowledged they lack Tarkovsky's resources (Soviet film budget, planted poppy field) 
    - **Camera tilt down at end of pan** is likely a crane move in the reference; their version will use tilt 

### Scene 8 (Old Woman)

- Shot in the **side-of-street location**, from the other side (Fujifilm photo reference confirmed this looks good) 
- Woman far enough in distance that old-age makeup is not critical for this shot; **clothing matters more** — same outfit as old woman 
- Costume decision: **granny-style dress/sundress** that hangs all the way down, with a small layer over it ; separate from the younger woman's clothes
- Costume for younger woman (jacket question): whether the old woman wears the same jacket as the younger woman was debated — could imply generational connection (mother's jacket handed down), but risks being too on-the-nose ; leaning toward **redressing the old woman entirely**
- Shoot timing: old woman shots should be **last of the day** since Holly's makeup process is irreversible for that session 
- Both locations (side-of-street and interview location) should be shot to have options in editing

## Equipment & Technical Decisions

- **Fisher dolly** (from Bad Dog rental): primary dolly for major shots; two seats — Sam operates camera, Shafi/Ishmam rotate on second seat or monitor screens 
- **Dolly transport problem:** Fisher may not be portable enough for stairs or deep forest; smaller rental dollies from Bad Dog are an option for those shots 
- **Shoulder rig:** Considered as fallback; may actually suit the slow-motion forest shots aesthetically if smooth enough, but both agreed it conflicts with the film's settled, Tarkovsky-like visual language 
- **Frame rate:** Standard 24fps for most shots; **30fps for forest/boy scenes** to allow subtle slow-down in post; 60fps considered too extreme for this film's tone 
- **Lens discipline:** All shots within a sequence must use the **same lens** to maintain consistency during pans and tilts 
- **Dolly tracks on grass:** Will be required for outdoor scenes; must plan accordingly 
- **Camera operator:** Sam expected to operate; Shafi and Ishmam to be on monitors; dolly push handled by dedicated operator
