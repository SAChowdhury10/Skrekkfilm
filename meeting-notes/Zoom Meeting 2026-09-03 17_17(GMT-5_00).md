## Scene-by-Scene Shot Breakdown

### Scene 3 → Scene 4 Transition

- End of Scene 3: Holly and Tyler are talking on the street; they look up at an unnamed object, and the camera begins to **tilt up** with them — but cuts before revealing what they're looking at. 
- The cut into Scene 4 should feel like **one continuous shot** by matching the tilt angle at the cut point, avoiding a jarring jump. 
- Scene 4 (boy in the woods): Camera starts tilted up, then **slowly tilts back down** to reveal the boy; a **high angle** is desired for the boy walking around in a static frame. 
- **Practical solution for high angle:** Use a ladder, or exploit the naturally hilly terrain so the camera operator is already elevated and the boy walks downhill — path must be cleared and tested for safety. 
- Timing challenge: The boy needs to begin walking before the tilt fully completes; a cue system will be used so he starts moving at the right moment. 

### Scene 5

- Returns to Holly and Tyler continuing down the street, this time shot **from the front**. 
- Camera eventually stops; **she walks off-screen**, he remains and watches her go. 
- Cut to a **static shot of her walking up stairs** — a location that already closely resembles the storyboard reference. 
- The man follows; she looks back (not directly at him); both **freeze momentarily** for a visual pause — no dialogue during this beat. 
- After the staircase moment, the scene moves to the area above the stairs for a complex **dolly/zoom shot** (see Lens Discussion below). 
- This shot alternates focus between the man and woman in a push-in pattern; on the final push toward the woman, **she is gone** — only smoke from his cigarette remains. 

### Scene 6 (Boy Finds the Bed)

- Opens **low angle**, forest visible in distance with the bed at the far end of the field; camera **tilts or pedestals up** to reveal the full landscape and the boy reaching the forest's edge. 
- Boy pauses at the forest edge, then walks all the way to the bed; **close-up of his hand touching the fabric**, likely a match cut from the wide. 
- Camera zooms out slightly to show the full bed in the field. 
- Boy gets on the bed and **starts jumping**; camera tilts up to frame just the apex of his jump, so he pops **in and out of frame** repeatedly. 
- Before the first jump, the frame should show only **empty sky**, giving the impression the camera just drifted upward before the boy appears. 
- After several jumps (approx. 5–6), on the expected 7th jump, **he doesn't appear** — frame holds empty, then tilts down to reveal him **on his knees on the bed**. 
- Camera pushes in on the back of his head; he turns to camera — **tears visible**. 
- From that close-up, a **~180° pan** sweeps back toward the forest, where the **outline/silhouette of the woman** is standing. 
- Whether this entire sequence can be executed as one continuous shot is a key open question; a cut between the jumping sequence and the close-up/pan may be necessary. 
- Low starting angle may be required so the tilt up only reveals the top of his head during the jumping portion. 

### Scene 7

- Static camera throughout; set against a **large open field backdrop**, possibly requiring the team to bring their own balls as props. 
- Holly and Tyler walk in, weave through the space, and **sit apart from each other**; conversation ensues. 
- Choreographed movement: she eventually moves to sit beside him; he turns toward her — all while dialogue continues. 
- This scene is flagged as a **good candidate for coverage** (closer insert shots) given the static wide. 
- Possible brief cut back to the boy after this scene — still being debated. 

### Scene 8 (Static Blocking Shot)

- Camera is completely static; the **actors move through the frame**. 
- Sequence of movements: woman enters from left, does a turn while delivering dialogue → man enters behind her → they adopt a **painting-style composition**, facing different directions while talking → woman exits frame → man does a full turn. 
- Leads into a compositional shot where the **man appears small, positioned just above the woman's shoulder** — achieved by placing him slightly downhill while she sits on a log or bench at a higher elevation. 
- Enough depth between the two actors is needed to sell the perspective. 

### Scene 9 (Dolly Push + Pan)

- Begins with a **two-shot** of both characters; camera **dollies in**, pivoting first toward the man, then toward the woman, then back. 
- Tilts down to her **book** (the man asks her to read from it at this point in the script). 
- While the tilt is down on the book, the man walks toward her and sits at a **lower level** so that when the camera **pans left**, his head is roughly level with the book. 
- Pan ends on a frame where he's seated beside her, head near book height — she is likely on a log, he is on lower ground. 

### Final Shot (Scene 8 / Closing)

- Long **pan across nature** — estimated **30–35 seconds** — then a **tilt down** to reveal Holly and Tyler sitting on the ground; voiceover plays during this. 
- Last image of the film: the **woman alone**, looking into the distance — a wide, still composition; team debating whether slight camera movement is needed but leaning toward static.

## Editorial & Coverage Strategy

Sam offered substantive editorial guidance that prompted the team to reconsider their approach to long takes:

- **Runtime concern:** Based on the current shot list, Sam estimates the film will run **longer than the team probably expects**; suggested targeting under 20 minutes, which Ishmam confirmed as acceptable. 
- **Long takes lock you in:** Spending significant time on a single long take means there is **nothing to cut to** if the take doesn't work or a performance moment falls flat. 
- **Coverage as insurance:** Sam advocated for shooting **wide + coverage (shot/reverse shot)** as a baseline so editors have options — e.g., cutting to a reaction shot if one performance beat isn't working. 
- **Grocery shopping analogy:** Sam compared shot listing to buying groceries — you buy more than you'll eat, because when you're cooking (editing), you want to choose what actually works, not be locked into what you planned. 
- **Selective long takes:** The team agreed that some scenes **must play out a certain way** for the film's meaning (particularly Scene 5's dialogue rhythm), but that other scenes — like Scene 7's static wide — are natural places to gather additional coverage. 
- **Ishmam's acknowledgment:** Agreed the team hasn't planned coverage sufficiently yet, but confirmed there is **room for it**, especially in Scene 5. 
- **Shafi's position:** Prefers having something over nothing — would rather have coverage available and not use it than find himself without options in the edit. 
- **Pacing tip from Sam:** Audiences don't need to see every transition (e.g., a character getting out of bed and walking to the bathroom); editors can **jump between states** freely and viewers will follow — useful for trimming the boy's longer walking sequences.
