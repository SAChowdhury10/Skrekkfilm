## Key Outcomes

Shafi and Ishmam conducted a detailed review of test footage shot at an outdoor location, evaluating composition, lighting, camera angles, lens choices, and movement for each planned shot across multiple scenes. The footage was assessed as generally promising in terms of composition and depth, but **harsh midday sunlight** was identified as the primary recurring problem affecting visual quality. Specific adjustments were agreed upon for nearly every shot, including camera height corrections, diagonal movement refinements, lens changes, and production design additions. A scouting trip with stand-in Holly on the **25th** was confirmed, and several open decisions around bench selection, leaf coverage, and exact framing remain to be finalized on the next visit.

## Lighting & Sun — Primary Technical Challenge

- **Midday sun (noon–3 PM) identified as the worst window** for shooting; harsh light creates unflattering shadows, washes out skin tones, and makes colors difficult to manage in post. 
- October sun does not rise as high as summer months, which may provide some natural relief compared to the June test shoot. 
- **Reference photo from June** (overcast morning) confirmed that diffused, cloudy light produces a dramatically superior look — the same location appeared far more cinematic without direct sun. 
- Strategy agreed: **wide shots should be scheduled early morning or late afternoon**; close/tight shots can be filmed during peak sun hours since a **12×12 diffuser** can be placed over the subject for inserts without casting a visible shadow in frame. 
- Wide shots cannot benefit from diffusion because the shadow of even a large diffuser would appear on screen at wider focal lengths. 
- **Day-for-overcast** technique was discussed (Hollywood equivalent: covering the entire sky with massive diffusion rigs); acknowledged as not feasible at this production scale. 
- Alex (colorist) is expected to apply color grading and grain, which may improve the look of some footage, but lighting issues are considered difficult to fully correct in post.

## Scene 1 — Park Bench Area: Shot-by-Shot Notes

### Shot 1 — Wide Establishing (Bench Area)

- Overall composition assessed as **pretty good**; trees in frame are a positive element. 
- **Horizon needs to be straightened** — the shot is visibly crooked, particularly visible along the concrete. 
- Camera height is working at current level; going higher causes subjects to look short due to downward tilt, and going lower risks showing too much unwanted foreground. 
- **Longer lens** would compress background and add depth but would require moving the camera further back, making a dolly move impractical. 
- Grass will likely **brown out further by October**, which is considered a positive aesthetic development — it was very green in June and has already begun to change. 

### Shot 2 — Over-the-Shoulder

- Composition and subject sizing assessed as **pretty good**; a slight increase in over-the-shoulder framing may be desired. 
- Leaves on the floor can be added as **production design** to enhance the autumnal feel. 

### Shot 2 / Front Shot — Pan Down to Notebook

- Movement currently executes a straight tilt down, then pans; **Decision: change to a diagonal movement** so that by the time the notebook is revealed, the camera has already panned to the left of frame. 
- Stated explicitly for notes: **Scene 1, Shot 2** — more diagonal movement so the pan is already underway when the book comes into frame. 

### Shot 3 — Character Turn

- Composition looks solid; greens in background read well. 
- **Decision: reduce the turn to a half-turn** rather than a full rotation — a half-turn was judged to look more natural and cinematic. 
- Shot 6 should be filmed from the **same setup as Shot 3** rather than changing the camera position, to maintain visual consistency. 

### Shot 4 — Elevated Angle (Currently Too High)

- Camera is currently positioned too high, causing subjects to look short and eliminating sky from the frame. 
- **Decision: lower the camera to get more sky** in the composition. 
- **Decision: keep the background wide open (no background blur)** — the detail in the background is preferred. 
- Lens used: noted as needing to be confirmed from production notes; 60mm referenced as a candidate, with 85mm discussed as an option. 

### Shot 4 — Close-Up / Diffuser Use

- Sun creates a shadow that obscures the subject's face in this tight shot. 
- **Solution: use a diffuser overhead** for this close shot specifically, as the frame is tight enough that the diffuser shadow will not appear. 
- Stand-in **Ishiko** was crouching on a bollard to achieve height during the test; noted that if **Holly** needs additional height, a cushion or booster could also be used. 

### Concrete Coverage — Production Design

- Large expanse of concrete is visible in several shots, particularly during the pan. 
- **The bench (to be brought on shoot day) will block a significant portion** of the concrete naturally. 
- Additional **fallen leaves** can be scattered to cover remaining visible concrete; team acknowledged this is hundreds of square feet and full coverage is impractical, but camera-visible areas can be prioritized. 

### Bench Selection

- Team needs to **purchase a bench via Marketplace**; no strong preference expressed for handle vs. no handle initially. 
- **Decision: lean toward no handle** — a bench with a side handle would make it difficult to execute the shot that tilts down from the character's side to the notebook. 
- Bench back: not completely open preferred.

*(Note: Renaming convention discussed — this shot may be better labeled* ***Scene 1, Shot 9 or 10*** *rather than Scene 2 or Scene 3, Shot 1, as it is thematically part of the continuous Scene 1 sequence until the boys' scene begins.)*

- The road/path with trees framing both sides is considered a strong compositional element worth preserving.

- **Paper floating in**: ideally the paper floats into frame naturally; a small weight on one side was suggested to influence its fall trajectory.

## Scene 5 — Staircase and Path Shots

### Scene 5, Shot 1 — Dirt Path Walking

- **Location: dirt path**; team discussed moving further down the path to areas with **more shade** to avoid sun problems. 
- Two versions of the walking arrangement exist: woman walks in front of man, or they walk side by side; **decision deferred** — both options to be tested, and it was noted that inconsistency between shots is not jarring. 

### Scene 5, Shot 1 — Staircase Options

- Two staircase locations were reviewed; **the second staircase (with more height and stairs running through the full frame) is preferred** compositionally. 
- The preferred staircase shot needs **better composition**: push in closer to the stairs, ensure the stairs fill the frame, and increase the distance between the two characters (woman should be further up the stairs). 
- Colors are problematic due to lighting but the location itself is considered solid. 

### Scene 5, Shot 3

- Camera needs to be raised to approximately **eye level** — currently too low. 
- Time of day must be carefully chosen for this shot. 

### Scene 5, Shot 5 — Reverse Shot (Guatemala Dialogue)

- **Decision: reframe the reverse shot** so it points more toward the trees rather than toward the stairs, maximizing tree coverage in the background. 
- **Decision: Holly (the actress) should pace forward and backward** during the dialogue — moving closer to and further from camera — while her primary eyeline is directed **out into the distance** rather than toward Tyler. 
- This movement creates a dynamic where she appears bigger (closer) then smaller (further) within the frame. 
- **Lens choice: select a focal length that captures her full body** when she is at the furthest point from camera, with legs cutting off only when she is closest. 
- Camera may need to be positioned further back, or Tyler's position cheated to the far side to create the necessary distance. 

### Scene 5, Shot 6

- **Decision: shoot from the opposite side** compared to the test footage. 
- Subjects should remain in the **bottom right of frame**. 
- Camera should **tilt up slightly**. 

### Scene 5 — Field/Open Area Shot

- Location looks too much like an empty field; **production design needed**: logs on the sides, or a fence laid down in the background, to give the space more visual life. 
- Sun is again a problem; otherwise the location is acceptable.

## Boys' Scene — Forest/Clearing Area

- Sam's handheld footage of the large forested area was reviewed; the depth and back-view composition were assessed as **very strong** — potentially the best-looking footage in the session. 
- **Key technical challenge**: the shot that pans to the woman is the same shot that must push in to a close-up of the boy (tears); **no cut in between** is planned. 
- A 35mm lens is wide enough to capture the wide establishing view but will require a **significant dolly push or physical camera movement** to achieve a close-up of the boy's face from the same setup. 
- A **dolly** is desired for this location; feasibility of bringing it to this outdoor spot needs to be confirmed. 
- The area where the **bed** will be placed needs to be **mowed** so the bed is visible above the grass line. 
- Trees and overgrowth in the boy's movement path may need to be trimmed so he can move freely and the acting is not impeded. 
- The "hole" quality of the clearing — where the boy appears to emerge from a depression into the bed — was noted as thematically resonant with **motherhood imagery**; the team wants to emphasize this visual by making the space look more like a hole.
