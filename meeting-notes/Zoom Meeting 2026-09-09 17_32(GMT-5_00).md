## Stylistic Tension: Tarkovsky vs. French New Wave

- **Core conflict identified:** Scene 1 was developed in a strongly Tarkovsky style (slow, ghostly, composed, axis-cutting), while scene 3's walking shot reads as French New Wave — specifically Godard/Breathless — creating a tonal discontinuity between the two. 
- **Shafi's framing:** He initially envisioned the film as "less weird" and more naturalistic overall; the Tarkovsky influence in scene 1 is stronger than he originally imagined, though he accepts it. 
- **Ishmam's framing:** French New Wave is not monolithic — *Breathless* and *Last Year at Marienbad* are both French New Wave but stylistically opposite; the film should aim for a blend closer to *Marienbad* in some places and *Breathless* in others, rather than a pure Tarkovsky imitation. 
- **Agreed principle:** The film should not read as "Tarkovsky shooting an Éric Rohmer script" — that mismatch would be a significant artistic failure. 
- **Tarkovsky-dominant scenes:** Scene 1 and the boy scenes are firmly in that register; the boy's scene in particular (digging, called by a sound) feels Bergman/Tarkovsky and cannot easily be read as naturalistic. 
- **French New Wave scenes:** The walking shot in scene 3 and the subsequent conversation/staircase sequences feel more like Rohmer or Godard. 
- **Resolution path:** Rather than overhauling scene 1 (already well-established), the team will rework scene 3's walking shot and potentially adjust the acting direction to reduce the jarring shift.

## Scene 3 Walking Shot — Primary Concern

- **The problem:** The walking shot (man turns, walks backward while talking to woman) reads as a "cool French New Wave character turn" — specifically compared to Jean-Paul Belmondo in *Breathless* — which clashes with the more composed, strange register of scene 1. 
- **Contributing factor:** Holly played the scene during rehearsal in a highly composed, *Last Year at Marienbad*-style manner (slow, deliberate walk, choreographed stop), which is how the reference material led her to interpret it — but this made the transition into the naturalistic walking conversation feel even more discontinuous. 
- **The dialogue itself is not the problem:** The lines (train delay anecdote, man on phone with wife, "I'm indifferent to fate," etc.) are not overly naturalistic in text — they are poetic enough to survive either register.  The issue is the visual language of the shot, not the words.
- **What must be preserved in any revised version:**
    1. Characters must be walking. 
    2. They must look up at something at the end of the shot. 
    3. The shot must transition smoothly from the boy scene immediately preceding it (boy walks away toward a sound; the walking shot was designed to mirror that motion). 
    4. Must remain simple to shoot — total shot count should stay at approximately 30. 
- **Options discussed:**
    - Shoot from the front instead of behind, to get more face and reduce the "backs of heads" problem that already exists throughout the film. 
    - Remove or soften the turn-and-walk-backward moment entirely. 
    - Keep the shot but direct the actors to play it less composed, less "cool," more incidentally naturalistic. 
- **Decision:** No change committed yet; both collaborators will think independently and reconvene Friday with proposals.

## Production Design — Bed Prop

- **Morgan's update:** She is researching bed frames on Facebook Marketplace; frames in the $50–$100 range are available. 
- **PVC pipe option:** Morgan proposed building the bed shape from PVC pipe painted to look like metal — lightweight, easy to manipulate, and potentially cheaper than a found frame. 
- **Wood concern:** Ishmam raised the risk that a wooden bed in that shape might read as a manger — a symbolic association they want to avoid being too on-the-nose. 
- **Shafi's counter:** A manger is typically viewed from the end, not the side, so the diagonal side-view of their bed shape likely would not trigger that reading strongly. 
- **Decision:** Give Morgan a few more days to find a metal option; wood remains a fallback if nothing suitable is found. 
- **WASI noted** as a potential resource for welding if metal fabrication is needed.
