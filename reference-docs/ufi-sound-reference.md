# Une Faible Impression — Sound Reference

## Our Sound Philosophy

***These are not rules. Just how we see it for this film.***

Sound is just another aspect of cinema. So are the screenplay, performances, cinematography, and production design, among others.

The goal of cinema is immersion across all of them. They should feel like one language.

A performance shouldn’t be saying something the cinematography isn't. Sound works the same way. It should belong to the same system.

Most contemporary films use dialogue to tell you where everyone is located. You know where people are standing. You know how far away they are. You hear the room, the field, the street, the forest. The sound constantly confirms the physical reality of the image. And through that kind of realism, you get immersion.

We are after something slightly different.

We still want immersion, but not through spatial realism. We want the voices to feel close in another way. Not because everything around them is carefully described, but because you stay with them as they speak and think.

The references below exist on a spectrum. Some are subtle, some are extreme. We will probably land somewhere in the middle. But they all share the same basic idea... Sound is NOT primarily describing space. It is expressing experience.

---

## Voice

Think of the dialogue as something that belongs to the people first and the location second.

Imagine sitting beside someone who is thinking out loud. You are not paying attention to the acoustics. You are listening to the thought. We would love the conversations between the MAN and the WOMAN to have that feeling.

Sometimes the camera moves away without the voice changing very much. That is okay. We do not need the sound proving where everyone is standing. We only need to stay close to them.

As of now, we are imagining all of the dialogue as ADR. Not because we are correcting production sound, but because we would like a little more freedom with where the voices live. The ADR should feel intentional rather than corrective. It should not call attention to itself, but it does not need to disappear completely into realism.

---

## Nature

Nature should always feel quietly alive.

Birds, wind, leaves, distant ambience, small movements in the landscape. These should be with us throughout the film without asking for attention. They are not simply describing the location. They are another presence in the scene.

There are moments where the landscape leans a little closer to the characters, especially when the wind picks up. Even then, we would rather it feel inevitable than dramatic.

## References

**Claire's Knee — Bench Conversation**

Probably the closest reference overall.

Listen to how the ADR sits just slightly beside the image. The voices stay close without feeling heavily tied to the space around them. The birds, the wind, and the footsteps are all equally present. Nothing is trying to impress us. We would love many of the conversations between MAN and WOMAN to live somewhere around here.

**The Sacrifice — Doctor Arrival**

This takes the same idea much further.

The voices begin to feel more like thought than speech. We probably will not go this far, but it is a helpful reminder that dialogue does not always have to describe physical space. The wind here is beautiful. Nature is not surrounding the scene. It feels like it is participating in it.

**The Sacrifice — Alexander and Maria**

Another example of voices existing a little outside the space they are spoken in.

Immediate and intimate without being tied too closely to the geography of the location. We would point especially to this for the quieter philosophical exchanges. Pay attention to the restraint. The footsteps in the grass are beautifully judged.

**Inside the Yellow Cocoon Shell — Church Conversation**

This may be the closest tonal reference. Also a contemporary reference (released in 2023).

The voices remain soft and consistent even as the camera pushes in, which gives the whole scene a calm, slightly dreamlike quality without ever feeling artificial. We are also pointing to this for the paper sounds. The handling of books, the turning of pages, all the small tactile details. Our film has notebooks, letters, maps, books, loose sheets, and writing throughout. We would love those sounds to quietly become one of the film's recurring textures.

**Teorema — Maid and Stranger**

Probably a little stronger than what we are imagining, but a reminder of how much weight small sounds can carry.

Listen to the birds, the footsteps, and especially the handling of books and paper. Those little sounds become part of the rhythm of the scene without ever feeling decorative.

**The Turin Horse — Return Home**

Our reference for the wind.

Not because we want weather this severe, but because the movement of the landscape and the movement of the sound feel completely connected. When the air moves, everything moves with it. That is the feeling we are after.
